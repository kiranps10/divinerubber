How to Choose the Right Entrance Mat for Your Building

6 October 2026 Divine Rubber Team 4 min read

ℹDraft for review — the Divine Rubber team will confirm this content and set a publication date before it goes live.

Most of the dirt and moisture inside a building comes in on people's shoes. The right entrance mat stops it at the door — protecting floors, reducing cleaning time and lowering the risk of slips. Here's how to choose one.

1. Start with the traffic

A home doorway might see a few dozen steps a day; a shop, station or hotel entrance can see thousands. Heavier traffic needs heavier mats. Thick rubber mats — such as hollow mats in 16 mm or 22 mm — stay flat, resist wear and won't creep across the floor.

2. Think about the weather

Outside the door, mats face rain, mud and sun. Choose a mat that lets water drain away. Hollow rubber mats have an open-hole design that wipes dirt off shoes and lets it fall through, while their studded bottom improves drainage and keeps the surface cleaner.

For covered or indoor areas, polypropylene mats are durable and weatherproof, and suit both inside and outside use.

3. Scrape first, then dry

The most effective entrances use more than one mat. A tough scraper mat outside removes grit and mud; a softer mat inside absorbs moisture. Coir mats are a good choice here — hard, flexible and hard-wearing, with excellent scrubbing action. Stud mats, covered with flexible rubber studs, are also effective at removing mud.

4. Match the mat to the floor underneath

On polished stone or tiles, slipping is the main concern. Look for a textured surface: octagonal-pattern hollow mats, for example, have a surface finish that doesn't slip even on highly polished floors.

5. Plan for cleaning

A mat only works if it's kept clean. Rubber mats can be lifted, shaken and washed, and open-hole designs make it easy to sweep or hose away trapped dirt.

Quick checklist

  • How many people use the entrance each day?
  • Is the mat outdoors, under cover or indoors?
  • Does water need to drain through the mat?
  • What floor surface will the mat sit on?
  • What size is the doorway — do you need several mats joined together?
