From Raw Rubber to Floor Mat: How Mats Are Made

16 October 2026 Divine Rubber Team 2 min read

A rubber mat looks simple, but it passes through several careful stages before it reaches a doorway, stable or kitchen floor. Here is how a mat takes shape at our facility in Eara, Neelamperoor, in Kerala's Alleppey District.

1. Raw materials

Our mats are made from RSS-4 rubber, hand-searched high-quality micro crumb and internationally branded chemicals. The facility sits about an hour from Kottayam, India's rubber town, close to the materials our mats are made from. Raw materials are stored on site, ready for production.

2. Mixing the compound

On the rubber mixing mill, rubber and additives are worked together into a uniform compound. Even mixing matters: it means every part of the finished mat behaves the same way underfoot.

3. Forming sheets and rolls

The sheeting line forms the compound into rubber sheets and rolls. Rolled products such as the Diamond Top Runner can later be cut to length for corridors and walkways.

4. Shaping and finishing

In rubber mat manufacturing generally, the pattern — open holes, studs, diamonds or coins — is given to the mat as it is shaped, and the rubber is cured to give it strength and elasticity. Each mat is then finished to its specification: square-edged, taper-edged or interlocking, as on the Interlocking Coin Mat.

5. Thickness inspection

Finished mats are checked against the order specification, including thickness — the difference between, say, a 16 mm and a 22 mm Universal Pattern mat matters to how it performs.

6. Storage, packing and dispatch

Approved mats are stored on site as organised stock, then packed and prepared for dispatch to customers in India and overseas. Our facility is about two hours from Cochin seaport and international airport.
